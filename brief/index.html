TAYLER ERBE / MODEL SELECTION · STRUCTURED EXTRACTION

The smallest context that works beat the biggest model.

A legislation pipeline needed one local model to pull fifteen structured fields out of every bill, and to keep doing it as the workload grew from one state to fifty. I tested four models across chunk sizes and concurrency on vLLM and a single NVIDIA L4, to make that choice once.

Short version: Llama 3.1 8B at 1,200-token chunks completed the schema most often at every chunk size tested, and held steady under load. A 14B model given whole bills did worst. One L4 covers one state's weekly volume; fifty states needs about three.

00 / THE QUESTION

Choose once,
for fifty states.

The pipeline reads every bill and fills a fixed schema: its goal, key provisions, who it affects, its likely impact, its dates and penalties. Changing models later means reprocessing everything, so the choice had to hold at fifty times today's volume.

THE TASK

Fifteen fields per chunk

Eleven descriptive fields and four short answers, returned as JSON. A chunk counts as complete (parse-OK) only when every field is filled.

THE SETTINGS

Chunk size and load

Bills are split into chunks before extraction. Chunk size and the number of requests in flight both change speed, and possibly quality.

THE TARGET

75,000 bills a week

The fifty-state goal, processed in an 18-hour window. Today's single-state load is about 11,000 bills a week.

FINDING 01

Bigger was worse

Qwen 2.5 14B, given each whole bill at once, completed the schema 45.0% of the time, below every 7B and 8B setting. With 5,000 tokens of input, it ran short of room to finish all fields. It was also the slowest model tested.

FINDING 02

Tight context won

Llama stayed on its own section of the bill at 1,200 tokens and completed the schema. Larger chunks pulled answers toward neighbouring sections.

FINDING 03

The stack changed the answer

An earlier evaluation on Ollama picked Mistral. Moving to vLLM, and testing Llama at a comparable size (8B instead of 3B), reversed it. The serving stack is part of the model choice.

Under load.

Raising concurrency from 1 to 24 changed quality by less than 0.6 points for every model, so tuning load is a hardware decision, not a quality one. What separated the models was the slowest response.

Why Mistral was ruled out. Its slowest requests grew almost fivefold under load, as long 3,000-token prompts queued behind each other for GPU memory. Qwen was fastest, but at its own best quality setting it completed the schema about 68 to 71% of the time against Llama's 92.5%, so its speed did not translate into more complete records.

02 / THE PROXY

Complete is not
the same as correct.

Parse-OK checks that every field is filled, not that the answer is right. So I tested whether it ranks settings the same way a quality review does.

ACROSS MODELS

It agreed

On 20 bills scored for accuracy, specificity and completeness, the quality review ranked the three models in the same order as parse-OK.

WITHIN A MODEL

It misled

Comparing two chunk sizes of the same model, parse-OK picked the wrong winner in 3 of the 4 pairs that had a clear winner.

WHY

Valid, but drifting

Larger chunks produced complete, well-formed JSON about the wrong section of the bill, or fields filled with "None". The format check passes; the content does not.

How quality was scored. The quality review used an AI reviewer (Claude Opus) with a fixed 0 to 2 rubric, run in two independent passes. Newer Lab studies do not let one model grade another, so treat these scores as screening evidence, not measured accuracy. Review details ↗

03 / LIMITS

What this does
not show.

These results choose a model and a configuration for one task. They are not a general ranking of the models.

  • The sweep used a controlled sample of 50 bills between 4,500 and 4,972 tokens long. The production mix of bill lengths has not been run yet, and the best chunk size depends on it.
  • Parse-OK measures whether the schema was completed, not whether the facts are correct. Quality scores came from an AI reviewer, not from labelled answers.
  • The 14B model's quality review was not completed; its result here is schema completion only.
  • Failed launches sometimes exited normally and wrote plausible files. They were caught by checking file sizes and run times, and every run was verified before counting.
  • Results are for vLLM 0.6.6 with AWQ quantization on an L4. Another serving stack or GPU can change the ranking, as the earlier Ollama evaluation showed.

Planned next: a run on the production corpus, adaptive chunk sizes, and a test on new hardware.
